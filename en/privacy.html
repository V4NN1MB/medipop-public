Privacy policy

Last updated: 27 November 2026. The Italian version is the original; this is a translation.

In short: the data you type into Medipop stays on your phone, encrypted. The developer does not receive it, cannot read it and has no server to keep it on. The only thing that can reach the developer is an e-mail that you decide to write.

Who the developer is

Medipop is developed by Vanni M., an individual developer. Contact for privacy and support: medipop.app@gmail.com.

The data you enter in the app

Medipop stores what you type: people and pets, illnesses, visits, treatments, vaccinations, medicines bought, notes and, from version 1.1, medical certificates with the photos of their pages, cards with the photos of both sides and, from version 1.2, prescriptions with the photos of their pages and the packs in the medicine cabinet with their photo. This is health data, so the app handles it with particular care:

  • it stays only on your phone, in an encrypted database (AES-256, SQLCipher);
  • the app has no internet access (it does not have the permission to connect), no account and no server;
  • there are no usage analytics, ads, tracking or third-party libraries that collect data;
  • the key that opens it is held by the phone's system (Android Keystore, iOS Keychain) and by a recovery code that only you know. The developer has neither the key nor the code.

Because you process this data for purely personal or household purposes (your family's records), the developer is not the one processing it, and data protection law does not apply to that use (Article 2(2)(c) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, GDPR; Article 2(2)(a) of the Swiss Federal Act on Data Protection, nFADP). The developer does not process personal data of the app's users, except for the e-mails described below.

Who it is for

Medipop is made for your own household: the people and pets you live with. Keeping your own family's health documents on your own phone is a personal use. Do not use Medipop for the documents of employees, of the athletes of a club or of patients. For another adult in the family, agree with them first.

Children

Medipop is meant for adults. Parents can enter their own children's health data in their own family records, as in a private notebook: the app is not aimed at minors, does not ask for their age and creates no accounts for them.

Photos of certificates and text reading (from version 1.1)

From version 1.1 you can keep medical certificates in Medipop and add pages to each one: you take a photo, choose a photo from the gallery or import a PDF.

  • Camera: on Android the phone's own camera app takes the picture and Medipop asks for no camera permission; on iPhone Medipop asks for the camera permission the first time you take a photo.
  • Storage: each page is saved as an image re-encoded without location or camera data (EXIF) and encrypted on the phone (AES-256-GCM, one key per page), in the same protected folder as the database. Temporary files are deleted immediately.
  • Text reading: Medipop reads the text of the pages on the phone, without internet: on iPhone with Apple's Vision framework, on Android with Google's ML Kit text recognition, whose model is inside the app. Nothing is sent to Apple, to Google or to the developer (the app has no internet permission). From the text Medipop suggests the type, the issue date, the validity, the absence dates and days and the doctor; you confirm or correct each value. The recognised text is stored in the encrypted database and can be searched in the diary.
  • "Share as PDF" builds a PDF of the pages and opens the phone's share sheet. Once shared, the PDF is outside Medipop and is not encrypted: whoever receives it can read it. The temporary PDF is then deleted from the phone.

A practical tip: for a sick-leave certificate, the employer only needs the version without the diagnosis (in Italy, the «attestato» or the protocol number). When you share the PDF, check which of the two you are sending. And keep the original: a photo is a copy.

Cards (from version 1.1)

From version 1.1 you can keep cards in Medipop: health card, insurance cards, pharmacy cards and others. When you add a card, Medipop keeps:

  • the photos of both sides, cleaned of hidden data (location, camera data) and encrypted like the certificate pages, one by one;
  • the text read from the photos;
  • the card details: holder, number, personal identification number, issuer, expiry date;
  • the content of the barcode.

Everything stays on the phone, in the encrypted database. Text and barcode are read on the phone, without internet: nothing is sent to anyone.

Pet microchips (from version 1.1)

To read a pet's microchip number you can take a photo of the pet passport or of the barcode label. The photo is used only to read the number and is not kept: only the number remains, and you check it.

Prescriptions (from version 1.2)

From version 1.2 you can keep prescriptions in Medipop: you take a photo of the pages or import a PDF. The same rules apply as for certificates: the pages are cleaned of hidden data and encrypted one by one; the text and the barcodes are read on the phone, without internet, and nothing is sent to anyone. Medipop suggests the date, the doctor, the prescription number, the validity and the medicines, and you confirm or correct them. When you share a prescription as a PDF, the PDF is outside Medipop and is not encrypted: whoever receives it can read it.

Medicine cabinet (from version 1.2)

For each pack, Medipop keeps: the medicine, whose it is, the expiry date, the lot, how much is left, where you keep it and, if you like, a photo, cleaned of hidden data and encrypted like the others. The small square code printed on the pack and the printed expiry date are read on the phone, without internet. Everything stays on the phone, in the encrypted database. Expiry notices are notifications of the phone with a generic text that does not name the medicine.

Package leaflets and official sites (from version 1.2)

Medipop contains no package leaflets, and the app itself has no network access. The "Package leaflet (official site)" button does not make the app connect: it asks the phone's browser to open the site of a public medicines agency (AIFA, Swissmedic, AEMPS, the French public medicines database or EMA), which is a third party. Before opening it, Medipop tells you so. That site receives what any site receives: the page asked for, which is the page of the medicine, and your IP address, and it handles them under its own rules. Medipop adds nothing about you or your family.

Backups

A backup is an encrypted .medipop file. You create it and you choose where it goes: the phone's save dialog, or the share sheet (WhatsApp, mail, Drive...). Medipop does not send it to anyone by itself. The file is encrypted: whoever receives it can open it only with Medipop and your recovery code or the family code. From then on the file is under the control of the service you chose (for example your mail or cloud) and its terms; the service can see that the file exists, its size and who you sent it to, but not its contents. Backups you create stay where you put them until you delete them.

The certificate and card entries travel in the encrypted .medipop backups like the other entries. From version 1.1 a backup can also carry the photos of certificate pages and cards: you choose, with a switch. The photos are encrypted inside the file like everything else. Someone without the code sees only that it is a Medipop backup, when it was made and roughly how many photos it holds: nothing of the contents.

From version 1.2 the encrypted backups also carry prescriptions, with their pages, and the packs of the medicine cabinet, with their photo.

The family code lets two parents' phones read each other's backups. Anyone who has the code and a backup can read the data: give it only to people you trust.

Sharing circles (from version 1.1)

A circle lets you share only part of the family with whoever you choose: any combination of people and pets, even a single one (for example just one child, or just the dog). You can have several circles, each with its own code and its own choice of whom to share.

  • A backup made for a circle holds only the people and pets you put in that circle and what belongs to them.
  • A pack in the medicine cabinet that belongs to the whole family is never in a backup made for a circle; the pack of a person or pet in the circle is.
  • It opens only with the circle's code, which is different from the family code.
  • Whoever has the circle's code can read those backups and can add or change the entries of those people. When you import their file, you see the changes and you confirm them.
  • What you write in the record of a shared person travels with it, free-text notes included.
  • If you leave a circle, this phone forgets the code. Files already sent stay readable by whoever has the code.

Deleting data

  • You can delete a single entry or a whole profile in the app.
  • If you uninstall the app, all data on that phone is deleted.
  • Backups you created stay where you saved or sent them: you have to delete them there.
  • The developer holds no archive of the app's data, so there is nothing to ask the developer to delete.

E-mails you choose to send

In the "More" menu you can choose "Problem or suggestion". Medipop does not send anything by itself: it prepares a message and opens your mail app, and you read it and send it, if you want, to medipop.app@gmail.com. You can also write to that address with a question without using the app.

What the message prepared by the app contains

  • The subject: "[Medipop version] title", with the title you write (at most 160 characters).
  • The text you write (at most 4000 characters).
  • A block of technical information, which you can see and which stays in the message: type (problem or suggestion), app version and build number, system (Android or iOS) and system version, language, the name of the screen you were on. No data you entered in the app.
  • Only if you tick "Attach the technical log": a short in-memory log (at most the last 3000 characters) with screen names and error types, scrubbed of passwords, e-mail addresses, recovery codes and long digit sequences. The app shows you what will be attached before you send. It contains no health data and nothing you typed into forms.

In addition, like any e-mail, the message arrives with your e-mail address and, if it is set in the account, your name. Your mail provider handles it under its own rules.

Please do not write health data (names, illnesses, medicines, diagnoses) or other personal data in the message, and do not attach files containing them. The message contains whatever you decide to write: if you put health data in it, it reaches the developer. If that happens by mistake, tell us and we will delete it.

What the developer does with e-mails

Your rights

For the e-mails you sent us you can ask for access, rectification, erasure, restriction, objection and, where applicable, portability: write to medipop.app@gmail.com. We will reply within one month. You also have the right to complain to a supervisory authority: in Italy the Garante per la protezione dei dati personali (garanteprivacy.it); in Switzerland the Federal Data Protection and Information Commissioner (FDPIC, edoeb.admin.ch).

Apple and Google

When you download the app, Apple (App Store) or Google (Google Play) process the data of your download and account under their own policies. The developer receives from them, at most, aggregated and anonymous statistics (installs, versions, countries). Medipop sends them no data and receives none from the app. The text recognition libraries of Apple (Vision) and Google (ML Kit) work on the phone, and Medipop sends them nothing.

This website

medipop.app is a website of static pages only, hosted by OVHcloud in France: no cookies, no analytics, no scripts, no content from other sites. Like every web server, OVHcloud's records the IP address and the requested page in its technical logs, for security and to run the service, and keeps them under its own rules. The developer does not use these logs for anything else.

Security

AES-256 encryption of the database and of backup files; the pages of certificates and prescriptions and the photos of cards and packs are encrypted like the database (AES-256-GCM, one key per photo); keys in the phone's protection system; app lock with fingerprint or PIN; no screenshots while the app is open (on Android) and no preview in recent apps. No system is perfect: on a rooted or jailbroken phone, or if someone knows your recovery code and has one of your backups, the data can be read. If you lose the recovery code and the phone, nobody, not even the developer, can give your data back.

Sources of the medicine list

The list of medicine names inside the app is built from public sources (see the home page). It works without connecting to any service.

Changes to this policy

If something changes, we will update this page and the date at the top. A change in how the app handles data would also be mentioned in the release notes.
